### Opening

Families here rarely lose land all at once. More often it happens slowly: the land is worth more on paper every year, the tax bill grows with it, and one day the next generation can't keep up.

I grew up on our family's ranch land in Kona, and I've helped manage family farmland most of my life. One of the quiet tools that keeps working land in working hands is the County's agricultural dedication. It doesn't make headlines, and it's easy to forget about until the tax bill arrives.

If your family holds ag-zoned land on this island, or you're about to inherit some, this is worth ten minutes. The rules changed recently, and 2026 has two deadlines you should know about.

### What ag dedication is, in plain words

Ag dedication is a promise. You agree to keep farming or ranching your land for a set number of years, and in return the County taxes the land on its value as a farm, not on what a developer or buyer would pay for it.

That gap can be huge. One Big Island owner writing for Hawaiʻi Life reported a land assessment of about $480,000 at market value, but only about $25,000 under dedicated agricultural use (Hawaiʻi Life). Your numbers will differ, but the scale of the savings is why this program matters to families.

**What changed.** In 2023 and 2024, the County Council tightened the rules to stop landowners with little real farming from claiming the break. Owners now have to show actual, ongoing agricultural use (Star-Advertiser). The old nondedicated program is being phased out. Anyone still in it had to move into a dedicated program in 2026 to keep their ag tax status (Aloha State Daily).

### Who qualifies and how to apply

Hawaiʻi County now offers three agricultural programs. The longer you commit, the bigger the break (Hawaiʻi Ag Business).

| Program | Commitment | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| 10-year dedication | 10 years | Established farms and ranches the family plans to keep; lowest assessment |
| 3-year dedication | 3 years | Families still deciding the land's long-term future |
| Community Food Sustainability | Renews every 5 years | Smaller food-growing operations |

**The basics to qualify** (County application form):

- The parcel must have County agricultural zoning. A garden on a residential lot doesn't count.
- The application asks whether the farm grosses at least $2,000 a year. The Kona Coffee Farmers Association suggests a prior-year GET form G-49 or IRS Schedule F as proof (KCFA).
- You need one supporting document: a farm plan (the County has a template), an NRCS conservation plan, USDA organic or food safety certification, at least $10,000 in farm expense or investment receipts within two years, or an agricultural conservation easement.
- You'll sketch the farm area, homesite and buildings on a map, and the County may inspect.

**Timing:** the normal deadline is September 1, and an approved dedication takes effect the following July 1. **For 2026 only, the County has extended the filing deadline to December 14, 2026**, because of Tropical Storm Lala (County Real Property Tax). If you missed September, you still have time this year.

Applications can be emailed to cohappraisal@hawaiicounty.gov, mailed, or dropped off at the Real Property Tax office.

### The legacy part: when land changes hands

Here's what families miss. A dedication is a commitment that comes with the land, not just with the person who signed it.

**Rollback taxes.** If the qualifying farm use stops, the use changes, or the dedication's conditions are broken, the County can charge rollback taxes: the difference between the farm assessment and the full market assessment, reaching back over past years (Living on Big Island). An heir who inherits a 10-year dedication and lets the coffee go wild may inherit that bill too.

**Selling.** A long-term dedication generally stays with the property after a sale unless the County releases it, so the buyer takes on both the farming commitment and the rollback risk. Before buying ag land, check the tax assessment and the title report for an existing dedication.

**Leaving the program.** When land comes out of an ag program, its assessment can jump straight to market value. In early 2026, the Council passed relief limiting yearly increases to 3% for a narrow group of longtime ag homeowners moving into the homeowner tax class. It runs through December 31, 2029 (Aloha State Daily).

#### New this year: ʻĀina Kupuna

For families on very old family land, the County has a brand-new program. ʻĀina Kupuna (ancestral land) lets qualifying families dedicate their land for 10 years and pay only the County's minimum tax, $200 a year when the bill was proposed (Hawaiʻi Tribune-Herald).

As proposed in June, eligibility required that the land:

- be 20 acres or less
- have been owned by the family since before January 1, 1926
- have carried more than $10,000 in property taxes over the past 10 years
- have at least one owner living in Hawaiʻi County

During the dedication, the land can't be sold to someone outside the line of descent, and it can't be used commercially except for agriculture. Families may need deeds, wills, trusts and genealogy records to prove descent (West Hawaii Today).

**The first-year deadline is December 31, 2026.** After that it moves to September 1 (County Real Property Tax). Confirm the final rules with the County before applying, since the bill could have changed before it passed.

### A checklist for families and heirs

- [ ] Look up the parcel on the County's property tax site and note its current assessment and program, if any.
- [ ] Check the title report for an existing dedication and its end date.
- [ ] If you're farming but not dedicated, file by **December 14, 2026** this year (September 1 in normal years).
- [ ] If the land has been in the family since before 1926, look into ʻĀina Kupuna before **December 31, 2026**.
- [ ] Keep farm records year-round: receipts, GET returns, photos, a simple farm plan.
- [ ] Before selling, subdividing, or changing how the land is used, ask what it means for rollback taxes.
- [ ] Make sure whoever inherits knows the land is dedicated and what that commits them to.

### Sign-off

Our family has been on this land a long time, and I've learned that keeping it is about paperwork as much as aloha. Ag dedication is one of the most practical tools a farming family has.

Rules and deadlines change, so confirm with the County Real Property Tax office, and talk with a CPA or estate attorney about your situation.
